Quick Reference

The whole lesson on one screen

CRAFT — five parts of a prompt that works. Name these five things and the output gets dramatically better.

  1. Context — the situation and your goal. "I run a small clinic launching evening telehealth hours." Leave out anything private.
  2. Role — tell the AI who to be. "You are a patient-communications writer." The single highest-leverage change you can make.
  3. Action — one clear, verb-driven task: write, rewrite, summarize, compare, list.
  4. Format — the shape of the output: "three captions under 30 words," "a 5-row table."
  5. Tone & examples — how it should sound, plus a sample to match.

Common pitfalls

  • Starting a brand-new chat to fix a draft — iterate in the same chat instead, so you keep the context.
  • Pasting real names, account numbers, or health details into a public/personal AI tool.
  • Skipping the Role — "help me with this" gets a shrug; a role reframes the whole answer.
  • Giving no Format, then being surprised the output is the wrong length or shape.

Better Prompt

CRAFT · specific
The prompt
You are a marketing copywriter. I run a small coffee subscription launching a decaf blend for evening drinkers. Write 3 Instagram captions, under 30 words each, warm and a little playful, each ending with a question.
AI output
1. "Your 9pm cup just got an upgrade — all the ritual, none of the buzz. What's your wind-down drink?"

2. "Decaf that doesn't taste like a compromise. Ready to reclaim your evenings?"

3. "Bold flavor, gentle finish, zero 2am regrets. Who's joining the evening club?"
✓ Role, Context, Action, Format, Tone — usable on the first try.

When the first draft misses, iterate — don't restart. "Make them shorter and drop the emojis. Keep the questions." A follow-up keeps all the context; a new chat throws it away.

Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Build a CRAFT prompt, one part at a time

We'll build one real prompt together — a short note telling existing patients the clinic is adding Saturday hours. Follow along in your own approved tool and swap in your details.

1 Open your approved AI tool and start a fresh chat +

Open the AI tool your organization approves and start a new chat — a fresh chat carries no leftover context from someone else's task. For anything work-confidential, use the business account, not a personal login.

What you'll see: an empty message box and, usually, a note of which account or workspace you're in. Confirm it's the approved one before you type anything.
Role variation: Clinical staff should default to the approved, HIPAA-covered tool for anything touching patient information. Front-desk and admin staff can use the general business tool for non-PHI tasks.
2 Lead with the Role and Context +

Start with who the AI should be (Role), then the situation and your goal (Context) — minus any private details. Type this as your first message:

Type this — Role + ContextYou are a patient-communications writer for a primary care clinic. We're adding Saturday morning hours, 8am to noon, starting July 12, and we want to let existing patients know.
What you'll see: the AI may acknowledge and offer to draft right away. That's fine — you'll hand it the rest in the next step.
Role variation: A scheduler might use "calm, concise front-desk coordinator"; a manager might use "operations lead briefing staff." Same structure, different voice.
3 Add the Action, Format, and Tone +

Now add the Action (one clear task), the Format (the exact shape), and the Tone (how it should sound, with a sample to match). Put together, your full CRAFT prompt reads like this:

The assembled CRAFT promptYou are a patient-communications writer for a primary care clinic. We're adding Saturday morning hours, 8am to noon, starting July 12, and we want to let existing patients know. Write a short announcement we can send as both a text and an email. Keep it under 90 words, friendly and reassuring, and end with one clear line on how to book. Match this opening tone: "A quick heads-up from your care team —"
What you'll see: a ready-to-use draft that fits the length, sounds the way you asked, and ends with a booking line — not generic filler.
Role variation: Admin staff often want a table or checklist; clinical staff more often want a plain-language patient summary. Name whichever shape fits your output.
4 Review the draft and iterate in the same chat +

Read the draft. If it's close but not quite right, reply in the same chat with a targeted fix — don't start a new chat, which throws away everything you've set up.

Type this — a follow-up, not a restartThis is close. Make it a touch warmer, drop the word "utilize," and add one line that walk-ins are welcome. Keep everything else.
What you'll see: the same announcement, revised — warmer, your edits applied, the rest intact. Repeat until it's right.
Checkpoint for every role: a person reviews the final wording before anything goes to patients. The AI drafts; you approve.
▸ Deep Dive: Why a public AI tool is like a public postcard Optional

Free and personal chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot on a personal account) may use what you type to improve their models, and you don't control where it goes. That's fine for a recipe; it's a problem for anything private.

The one-line test

If you wouldn't post it on a public bulletin board, don't paste it into a public AI tool.

Three habits that keep you safe without slowing you down

Swap in placeholders. "A patient," "Company X," "my coworker" instead of real names and numbers — the AI doesn't need the real ones to help.

Strip before you paste. Rewriting an email? Drop the signature, phone numbers, and account IDs first — paste only the part you need rewritten.

Use the approved tool for sensitive work. A provided business AI account has different data protections than a personal login.

Never paste these into a public/personal tool

Passwords and login codes; full Social Security or financial account numbers; other people's personal details (names, contact info, health information); and confidential company, client, or patient information.
